# Lesson Plan — MS Paint → Illustrator

Eight core sessions, 20–30 minutes each, one idea per session. Sessions are a
menu, not a contract: reorder or stretch them across multiple sittings
whenever Codie's energy says so. Every session ends with something finished
enough to feel good about.

The arc is built around Codie's existing style — black ink-style silhouettes,
thorny vines, wreaths, trees, crystals, repeated motifs — so each lesson
produces art that looks like *theirs*, just with new powers.

---

## Session 1 — Nothing is stuck down

**Idea:** in Illustrator, everything you draw stays a separate object you can
pick up later. Marks never dry onto the canvas.

- New document (any preset), quick tour of only: toolbar, canvas/artboard,
  the fill/stroke squares at the bottom of the toolbar.
- Draw shapes: Rectangle (`M`), Ellipse (`L`), `Shift` for perfect ones.
- Selection tool (`V`): move, resize, rotate, recolor things *after* drawing
  them — the moment that separates Illustrator from Paint.
- Fill vs stroke in one sentence: "the inside color and the outline color."
- Save the file into `projects/` as `.ai` — explain it's the master copy.

**Exercise:** build a crystal (like the ones in their Paint art) from a few
straight-edged shapes. Overlap, resize, rotate. Nothing needs to be perfect —
everything can be nudged afterwards, that's the lesson.

**Shortcuts:** `V`, `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+S`

---

## Session 2 — Make it feel like Paint

**Idea:** the pen still works like a pen. Illustrator can feel like Paint
when you want it to.

- Blob Brush (`Shift+B`) — paints filled shapes, the closest thing to
  Paint's brush. Draw with it freely.
- Set up pressure together: Brushes panel → double-click the calligraphic
  brush → Size: **Pressure**, variation a few points. Now strokes taper like
  real ink — show how a fast tapered stroke *is* a thorn.
- Eraser (`Shift+E`) — carve into blob shapes, just like Paint's eraser.
- Zoom (`Ctrl +`/`Ctrl -`, or `Alt`+scroll) and show that nothing pixelates.

**Exercise:** freehand a thorny vine spiral exactly the way they'd draw one
in Paint — Blob Brush for the stem, quick tapered flicks for thorns. Save it;
Session 4 turns this into a superpower.

**Shortcuts:** `Shift+B`, `Shift+E`, `Ctrl +`/`-`

---

## Session 3 — Fixing without redrawing

**Idea:** the superpower Paint never had — reshape a line after it's drawn.

- Direct Selection (`A`): the little dots (anchor points) a line hangs off;
  drag them to bend what's already there.
- Smooth tool (under Pencil): tidy a wobbly stroke by drawing over it.
- Group (`Ctrl+G`) related pieces so they move as one.
- Fearless experimenting: `Ctrl+Z` is effectively unlimited.

**Exercise:** take Session 2's vine and improve it without redrawing —
fatten the stem, re-curve the spiral, drag a thorn longer. Then group it.

**Shortcuts:** `A`, `Ctrl+G`

---

## Session 4 — The thorn brush (showstopper)

**Idea:** draw a motif once, and Illustrator repeats it along any line
forever. This is why their style belongs in this program.

- Clean up one short thorn-vine segment (from Session 2/3 work).
- Drag it into the Brushes panel → **Pattern Brush**.
- Now draw any curve with the Paintbrush — it comes out as briar vine.
- The payoff: draw a circle with the Ellipse tool, apply the brush —
  **instant thorn wreath**, like the one in their Paint art but perfectly
  round and resizable.
- Try a spiral, a heart, their initial. Same brush, every shape.

**Exercise:** a thorn wreath + one wild-card shape wrapped in briars.
Export a PNG to `exports/` (first export — File → Export → Export As).

**Shortcuts:** `B`, `L`

---

## Session 5 — Trees, texture, and layers

**Idea:** their silhouette trees, rebuilt with the right tools — and layers
to keep a busier piece under control.

- Trunk and branches: Blob Brush with pressure, thick-to-thin strokes.
- Stippled canopy: make a **Scatter Brush** from a small dot/leaf cluster,
  then scribble it over the canopy area — instant texture like their
  stippled treetops.
- Layers panel (the one new panel today): `trunk` / `canopy` layers; lock
  one, work on the other, hide/show to compare.

**Exercise:** one windswept silhouette tree in their style, on two layers.

**Shortcuts:** `Shift+B`, layer lock/hide clicks

---

## Session 6 — Bring the old art in (Image Trace)

**Idea:** their existing Paint art can migrate — Illustrator converts it
into vectors automatically, and black-on-white art traces beautifully.

- File → Place a piece from `my-paint-art/` onto the artboard.
- Image Trace (Window → Image Trace, preset **Black and White Logo**),
  tweak threshold, then **Expand** to get real editable vectors.
- Zoom way in: jagged pixels are gone, edges are crisp. Compare against the
  placed original side by side.
- Clean up with Direct Selection / Eraser; delete stray specks.

**Exercise:** trace their favourite Paint piece, clean it up, export a PNG
next to the original in `exports/` for the before/after.

**Shortcuts:** `A`, `Ctrl+Shift+P` (Place)

---

## Session 7 — Symbols and the sticker sheet

**Idea:** their pages of repeated motifs, without redrawing anything.

- Symbols panel: drag a finished motif (wreath, tree, crystal) in — now
  drag out as many copies as wanted; resize/rotate each freely.
- Update the symbol once → every copy updates.
- Arrange a sheet: multiple motifs, varied sizes, like their Paint pages —
  this is genuinely how sticker sheets and tattoo-flash sheets get made.
- Teaser if energy allows: Object → Pattern → Make turns a motif into
  endless repeating wallpaper.

**Exercise:** a one-artboard flash/sticker sheet from their motif library so
far. Export it.

**Shortcuts:** `Shift+S` (Symbol Sprayer, optional), drag-from-panel

---

## Session 8 — Smooth curves (Curvature, then Pen)

**Idea:** the tool everyone finds hardest, saved for last and made gentle.

- **Curvature tool** (`Shift+~`) first: click points, curves happen —
  friendly version of the Pen.
- Pen tool (`P`) basics only: click for corners, click-drag for curves.
  Frame it as "for when you want a line smoother than your hand."
- Where it pays off in their style: long clean briar stems, bird silhouettes,
  crystal facets.

**Exercise:** trace one smooth motif (a bird or crystal) with the Curvature
tool over a dimmed template layer (place original, lower opacity, lock).

**Shortcuts:** `Shift+~`, `P`

## After the core eight

- **Describe & draw commissions** — Codie describes anything; break it into
  a build order and coach it through. This becomes the default ongoing mode.
- **Repeating pattern project** — Object → Pattern → Make with the briar
  motifs: wrapping paper, phone wallpaper, fabric-style repeats.
- **Print/share pipeline** — Export for Screens, SVG vs PNG, what to send
  to a sticker printer or t-shirt service.
- **Color, when they want it** — swatches, Live Paint (`K`) coloring-book
  workflow, Recolor Artwork to try whole palettes in one click.
- **Weekly challenge** — one motif prompt per week ("a key wrapped in
  thorns"), added to a running challenge artboard.
